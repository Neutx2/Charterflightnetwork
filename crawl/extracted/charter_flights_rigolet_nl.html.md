The remote coastal community of Rigolet is located east of Goose Bay on Hamilton Inlet at the entrance to Lake Melville. The town of just over 300 residents, is served by a public use airport which has a single gravel runway 2,496 feet long.

Rigolet is a small Inuit community located on the northern coast of Labrador, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada.

##### Location:

Rigolet is situated on the northern shore of Hamilton Inlet, which is part of the Labrador Sea. It is located approximately 350 kilometers northeast of Happy Valley-Goose Bay, one of the larger towns in Labrador.

##### History:

Rigolet has a long history of Indigenous occupation, with the Inuit people traditionally inhabiting the region. European contact in the area began in the 18th century with the arrival of European fur traders and missionaries. Inuit-European interactions influenced the cultural and social dynamics of the community.

##### Transportation:

Rigolet is accessible primarily by boat or by air, as there are no roads connecting it to other communities. The town has a small airstrip that provides regular air service to other parts of Labrador and Newfoundland. Additionally, Rigolet has a wharf that serves as a transportation link for marine traffic.

##### Community Life:

Rigolet has a close-knit community with a strong sense of cultural identity and pride. Residents often participate in community events and gatherings, including traditional ceremonies, festivals, and celebrations. The town's cultural heritage is preserved through storytelling, music, and other cultural practices.

##### Services and Amenities:

Despite its small size and remote location, Rigolet offers basic services and amenities to its residents. These include schools, healthcare facilities, a community center, and a local store. The town is also served by government services and agencies.

##### Challenges:

Like many remote communities in Labrador, Rigolet faces challenges such as limited access to essential services, economic opportunities, and infrastructure. The harsh climate and geographic isolation can also present logistical challenges for residents.
