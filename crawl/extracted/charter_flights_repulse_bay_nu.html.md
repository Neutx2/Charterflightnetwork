##### Location of Naujaat / Repulse Bay

###### We Arrange both Jet and Turbo Prop Aircraft

Naujaat, also known as Repulse Bay, is a small but culturally rich community located in the Kivalliq Region of Nunavut, Canada. Situated directly on the Arctic Circle, Naujaat lies along the northwestern coast of Hudson Bay, where the bay meets the Melville Peninsula. The name "Naujaat" means "seagull nesting place" in Inuktitut, a reference to the abundant seagulls that nest along the rocky shoreline. Known for its breathtaking landscapes, Naujaat offers stunning views of Arctic waters, icebergs, and expansive tundra. The town is a hub of Inuit culture, offering visitors a unique opportunity to experience traditional hunting, fishing, and community life.

Naujaat's remote location and the limited infrastructure make air travel the most practical and efficient way to reach the community, whether for tourism, business, or exploration.

##### Scheduled Airlines

Naujaat Airport (YUT) is served by regional carriers, primarily Calm Air and Air North, which connect the community with other parts of Nunavut and southern Canada. Calm Air offers flights from key hubs such as Winnipeg, Rankin Inlet, and Churchill, making it one of the primary options for scheduled passenger services. Air North also provides connections, often linking Naujaat with Iqaluit and Yellowknife. Both airlines operate reliable, year-round flights using aircraft like the ATR 42 and Dash 8, designed to handle the extreme Arctic conditions. Flights may be affected by severe weather, so travelers should allow for potential delays, especially in winter.

##### Naujaat / Repulse Bay Airport

Naujaat Airport (YUT) is a small but essential transportation hub, serving the remote community of Naujaat (Repulse Bay) in the Kivalliq Region of Nunavut. The airport is located just 2 kilometers from the town center, making it easily accessible by a short vehicle ride or even a brisk walk. The airport features a modest terminal building with basic passenger facilities, including a waiting area, check-in counters, and restrooms. Though limited in size, it provides necessary services for travelers and local residents.

The runway at Naujaat Airport is 3,500 feet (1,067 meters) long, made of gravel and suitable for handling small to medium-sized aircraft that are designed for Arctic conditions. Regular passenger services are operated by regional airlines like Calm Air, using aircraft such as the ATR 42 and Dash 8. These turboprop planes are ideal for short runway operations and can handle the region's challenging weather.

##### Air Cargo

Due to its remote location on the Arctic Circle, Naujaat (Repulse Bay) relies heavily on cargo flights to bring in essential supplies and goods year-round. These flights play a critical role in supporting the local community by transporting everything from food and medical supplies to construction materials and household goods.

NorthStar Air offers reliable cargo services from Thompson, Manitoba, utilizing its fleet of robust, all-weather aircraft. NorthStar Air's turboprop planes, like the ATR 72-500F and the Basler BT-67 DC3, are both designed for Arctic conditions, handling both the short gravel runway at Naujaat Airport and the challenging weather. These cargo flights ensure timely delivery of vital supplies, making them an essential link for the region's industries and residents.

##### Outdoor Adventures & Activities

Wildlife enthusiasts will be thrilled by the variety of wildlife tours available in Naujaat. The area is known for its abundant marine life, including beluga whales that can often be spotted in the summer months. Local guides offer whale watching tours, providing a chance to observe these magnificent creatures up close. For the more adventurous, polar bear viewing tours are a popular option, offering a safe and unforgettable way to see these apex predators in their natural habitat.

In addition to wildlife tours, visitors can enjoy fishing excursions, bird watching, and hiking along the tundra, where they can spot Arctic foxes and caribou.

##### Crew Rotation Flights

Group charters and crew rotation flights are a crucial part of transportation services in Nunavut, enabling efficient movement of personnel, workers, and specialized groups between remote communities and major urban centers. These charters cater to mining companies, exploration teams, and construction crews who require reliable and timely travel for projects across the Arctic region.

From key hubs like Winnipeg and Thompson, Manitoba, as well as Montreal and Quebec City, customized charter flights connect directly to communities throughout Nunavut, including Rankin Inlet, Naujaat, Baker Lake, Iqaluit, and more. Using versatile aircraft such as the Dash 8, ATR 42, and Beechcraft 1900, charter services can adapt to the unique requirements of the Arctic terrain, ensuring safe and efficient travel even to airstrips with shorter runways.

##### Weather & Suggested Clothing

Naujaat (Repulse Bay) experiences the harsh climate of the Arctic, with a stark contrast between summer and winter seasons. During the summer (June to August), temperatures typically range from 5°C to 15°C (41°F to 59°F). While the long days and midnight sun can bring mild and pleasant weather, cool winds from Hudson Bay are common. Be prepared for changing conditions by packing light layers, including a windproof jacket, sweater, and water-resistant gear.

In the winter months (November to April), Naujaat faces extremely cold temperatures, often between -30°C and -40°C (-22°F to -40°F), with strong winds that intensify the chill. Visitors should expect freezing conditions and frequent snow. To stay safe and comfortable, pack thermal layers, a heavy parka, windproof gloves, a warm hat, and insulated boots rated for severe cold.
