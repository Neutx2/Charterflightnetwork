### Turboprop Charters

Pickle Lake is a key hub for turboprop aircraft, offering a wide selection of options suited for both passenger and cargo operations into the Far North. Operators such as North Star Air and Wasaya Airways base aircraft here, including versatile 8–9 passenger Pilatus PC‑12s and Cessna Caravans that efficiently connect First Nations communities to essential services. For larger missions, North Star Air operates specialized cargo turboprops like the ATR 72‑500 freighter with an 18,000‑lb payload, and the Basler BT‑67 (a modernized DC‑3) carrying up to 10,000 lbs. If your requirement involves moving bigger groups, Dash 8‑100 and Dash 8‑300 series aircraft can seat 37–50 passengers, making them ideal for mining companies who require regular crew rotations, community travel, or special events. These helicopters are fully equipped to support a broad spectrum of missions, including wildfire suppression, mining and exploration transport, power line and utility survey, search and rescue, environmental monitoring, and even film crew logistics. ## Pickle Lake Outdoor Adventures

At the northern end of Highway 599, **Pickle Lake Airport** (YPL) serves as the gateway to Ontario’s Far North. It supports regional turboprops, light business aircraft, and float‑plane or helicopter connections to communities and camps beyond the road network. Travelers should plan ahead for rental cars and ground shuttles due to limited local inventory.

### Gateway to Wilderness & Fly‑In Adventures

Pickle Lake is widely recognized as the gateway to Ontario’s Far North and offers direct charter access to a variety of remote fishing and wilderness destinations. Guests can connect here for float plane flights into exclusive lodges and outpost camps that are otherwise inaccessible. One of the region’s highlights is **Birch Bark Lodge**, a classic fly‑in lodge located north of Pickle Lake that provides full‑service packages for anglers and adventurers. Another renowned destination is **North Caribou Lake**, one of the most productive fisheries in the area. Although no full lodge operates here, several outpost camps—including those managed by **Pickle Lake Outposts** and their affiliated **Osnaburgh Airways**—offer comfortable accommodations right on the water. North Caribou is especially famous for producing trophy northern pike, making it a bucket‑list destination for serious anglers. Whether your interest is fishing, paddling, or simply exploring the boreal wilderness, Pickle Lake serves as the essential launch point for fly‑in adventures.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### How do I clear Canadian customs when flying to Pickle Lake?

International flights must first clear Canadian customs at Thunder Bay before continuing on to Pickle Lake. After clearing customs, passengers connect with a direct charter flight north.

### Can I connect directly to fishing lodges from Pickle Lake?

Yes. Float plane operators based at Pickle Lake provide direct transfers to lodges and outpost camps, including Birch Bark Lodge and North Caribou Lake outposts.

### What types of aircraft are available for Pickle Lake charters?

Options range from light jets operated by Fast Air, to turboprops such as the PC‑12, Cessna Caravan, ATR 72‑500 freighter, and Dash 8, as well as float planes and helicopters.

### Is Pickle Lake known for its mining and exploration opportunities?

Absolutely. Mining and exploration companies regularly charter flights into Pickle Lake to move crews, equipment, and supplies. Aircraft are available for both passenger and heavy cargo transport.

### What makes Pickle Lake popular with anglers?

Pickle Lake is the launch point for some of Ontario’s best fly‑in fishing. North Caribou Lake, in particular, is famous for producing trophy northern pike and outstanding walleye fishing.
