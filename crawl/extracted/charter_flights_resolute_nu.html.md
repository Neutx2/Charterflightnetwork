##### Resolute, Nunavut: A Gateway to the High Arctic & Quttinirpaaq National Park

###### We Arrange both Jet and Turbo Prop Aircraft

Resolute, known as "Qausuittuq" in Inuktitut, meaning "place with no dawn," is one of Canada's northernmost communities, located on Cornwallis Island in Nunavut. It lies along the Arctic Circle, making it a key hub for Arctic exploration, scientific research, and polar adventure. This remote hamlet is surrounded by a stark and stunning landscape, characterized by expansive tundra, glacial features, and icy waters that are frozen for much of the year.

The geography of Resolute is defined by its rugged Arctic environment. The island's terrain consists of low, rolling hills, rocky outcrops, and permafrost-covered ground. Resolute Bay, from which the community takes its English name, opens to the Northwest Passage, historically significant as a route sought by explorers. The area is also rich in Arctic wildlife, including polar bears, seals, narwhals, and migratory birds, making it a haven for wildlife enthusiasts and researchers.

Accessible primarily by air, Resolute serves as a critical logistics hub for Arctic expeditions and research, with its strategic location providing entry to the High Arctic's untouched wilderness. For adventurers, scientists, and those drawn to the beauty of the polar regions, Resolute offers a unique and unforgettable Arctic experience.

##### The Resolute Airport

Resolute Airport (IATA: YRB) serves as a vital transportation hub for Resolute, Nunavut, and the surrounding Arctic region. It is a critical link for passenger travel, cargo deliveries, and Arctic research expeditions, facilitating access to one of the most remote areas in Canada.

The airport features a gravel runway that is 1,981 meters (6,500 feet) long, capable of accommodating various aircraft types, including smaller passenger planes like the Dash 8 and Twin Otter, as well as cargo aircraft designed for Arctic operations. These planes transport essential supplies, equipment, and passengers year-round, ensuring the community remains connected despite its isolation.

While the airport is not operational 24 hours a day, its hours of operation are flexible and adjusted to accommodate scheduled flights and weather conditions, which can change rapidly in the Arctic. Resolute Airport is a key lifeline for the region, supporting both the community and vital Arctic operations.

##### Cultural Experiences and Inuit Activities

Resolute, or "Qausuittuq," meaning "place with no dawn," offers visitors a unique opportunity to immerse themselves in the rich cultural heritage and traditions of the Inuit people. Located in the heart of the High Arctic, this remote community is steeped in history and resilience, showcasing the enduring connection between the Inuit and their environment.

Visitors to Resolute can experience the Inuit way of life through cultural activities and storytelling sessions hosted by local residents. These stories often recount the Inuit's deep knowledge of the land, ice, and wildlife, as well as their history of survival and adaptation in one of the world's harshest climates. Guests can learn about traditional hunting methods for seal, narwhal, and caribou, as well as techniques for building igloos, which serve as temporary shelters during hunting expeditions.

##### Air Cargo Flights

Resolute, Nunavut, relies heavily on cargo flights to supply the community with essential goods and materials throughout the year. Located in the remote High Arctic, Resolute is inaccessible by road, making air transportation a vital lifeline for residents, researchers, and businesses.

Cargo flights to Resolute are provided by dedicated operators experienced in Arctic logistics, ensuring reliable service despite challenging weather and terrain. Two primary carriers, North Star Air and Perimeter Aviation, play a critical role in delivering cargo to the region from southern hubs in Manitoba.

##### Explore Quttinirpaaq National Park

Quttinirpaaq National Park, located on the northern tip of Ellesmere Island, is the second-largest national park in Canada and one of the most remote wilderness destinations on Earth. Resolute, Nunavut, serves as a popular starting point for visitors embarking on their journey to this Arctic wonder. From Resolute, travelers typically take chartered flights to Tanquary Fiord or Lake Hazen, the main access points to the park.

Quttinirpaaq, meaning "top of the world" in Inuktitut, offers breathtaking landscapes of glaciers, fjords, icefields, and rugged mountains. Visitors can expect to see dramatic Arctic vistas, ancient rock formations, and pristine environments untouched by human development. Wildlife enthusiasts may spot Arctic wolves, muskoxen, and Peary caribou, while the skies are often graced by migratory birds.

Visitors are not required to have a local Inuit guide to enter the park, but hiring one is highly recommended for safety and to enrich the experience with cultural and ecological knowledge. Guides offer insights into Inuit heritage and survival skills that have been passed down for generations.

##### Crew Rotation Flights / Group Charters

Resolute, Nunavut, serves as a vital hub for Arctic travel, research, and industrial operations, making group charter and crew rotation flights essential for connecting the community with major Canadian cities. These flights offer customized solutions for transporting groups, personnel, and supplies to and from Resolute efficiently and reliably.

Group charter flights are available from major destinations such as Winnipeg, Thompson, Montreal, and Quebec City. These services are ideal for research teams, corporate groups, or government delegations traveling to Resolute for Arctic expeditions, exploration projects, or community support initiatives. Aircraft such as the Dash 8, ATR-72, and other specialized planes ensure comfort and safety while accommodating passengers and cargo.

##### Wildlife Tours and Polar Bear Photography

The Arctic waters and tundra around Resolute are teeming with wildlife. Guided tours provide the chance to observe polar bears in their natural habitat, offering incredible opportunities for photographers to capture these majestic creatures against the dramatic Arctic backdrop. Additionally, visitors may spot seals, Arctic foxes, and migratory birds during the summer months.

##### Northern Lights Viewing

Resolute's location above the Arctic Circle offers ideal conditions for viewing the Northern Lights. From late autumn through early spring, the aurora borealis paints the night sky with vivid greens, purples, and pinks, creating an unforgettable spectacle.
