Location of Grise Fiord
We Arrange both Jet and Turbo Prop Aircraft

Grise Fiord, is one of the most northerly communities in Canada and lies on the southern coast of Ellesmere Island in Nunavut’s vast Arctic Archipelago. Nestled within a stunning fjord, the community is surrounded by towering mountains, ice-covered waters, and sweeping Arctic tundra. Known as "Aujuittuq" in Inuktitut, meaning "place that never thaws," Grise Fiord is home to breathtaking natural beauty and a small, resilient population that thrives in one of the harshest climates on Earth. The geography of Grise Fiord is characterized by dramatic contrasts. The fjord itself is a deep, glacially carved inlet flanked by rugged peaks that rise steeply from the sea. The surrounding landscape is a mix of ancient rock formations, expansive ice fields, and coastal tundra, creating a surreal, otherworldly environment. Icebergs drift through the waters, and the area is a haven for Arctic wildlife, including polar bears, seals, and narwhals. The midnight sun in summer and long polar nights in winter make it a place of extraordinary natural rhythms.

The Grise Fiord Airport

Grise Fiord Airport (IATA: YGZ) serves as the lifeline for the remote Arctic community of Grise Fiord, located on Ellesmere Island in Nunavut. Set amidst the dramatic landscape of the High Arctic, this small airport plays a critical role in connecting Grise Fiord to the rest of Canada, ensuring the delivery of essential goods, mail, and transportation for residents and visitors.

The gravel runway at Grise Fiord Airport is 1,675 feet (511 meters) long, making it suitable for smaller, specially equipped aircraft capable of handling short, unpaved airstrips. Aircraft such as the Twin Otter which  frequently operate here, carrying passengers and cargo. These planes are specially designed for Arctic conditions, with capabilities for takeoffs and landings on challenging terrain and in extreme weather. Cargo flights often deliver crucial supplies, from food and fuel to building materials.

Activities and Adventures
Wildlife Tours:

Grise Fiord’s remote location makes it an excellent spot for viewing Arctic wildlife. Guided tours offer opportunities to see polar bears roaming along the icy shores, narwhals gliding through the frigid waters, and seals basking on ice floes. Migratory birds are also abundant during the summer months.

Whale Watching

The waters surrounding Grise Fiord are home to narwhals, beluga whales, and even bowhead whales. Local guides can take visitors on boat tours to witness these majestic creatures in their natural habitat.

Hiking and Exploring:

The rugged terrain offers incredible hiking opportunities. Trek through glacial valleys, marvel at ancient rock formations, and take in sweeping views of fjords and icebergs.

Inuit Culture & Heritage

Grise Fiord is also a place of modern resilience. Established in the 1950s during a government relocation program, its residents have maintained their cultural identity while adapting to life in a remote Arctic outpost. Today, visitors can engage with the local community to hear stories of survival, migration, and connection to the land, passed down through generations.

The history of the Inuit people is deeply rooted in the Arctic landscape. Inuit ancestors adapted to this challenging environment by developing unique skills and practices, such as building igloos, navigating by the stars, and crafting clothing from animal hides to withstand extreme cold. Visitors to Grise Fiord can witness these traditional practices firsthand, learning about hunting techniques, seal and caribou preparation, and the artistry of Inuit carvings and tools.

Helicopter Sightseeing Flights

Sightseeing flights from Grise Fiord offer a unique perspective on the High Arctic’s majestic beauty. Passengers can soar over towering glaciers, dramatic fjords, and ice-covered waters teeming with wildlife. These flights provide opportunities to see polar bears, narwhals, and seals in their natural habitats, as well as stunning views of the floe edge where ice meets open sea. Each flight is led by experienced pilots who provide insight into the region’s geography and ecology.

Helicopter Support for Industry and Research

Helicopters play a vital role in supporting mining, exploration, and natural resource industries in the Arctic. They are equipped to transport personnel, supplies, and equipment to remote sites, facilitating geological surveys and mineral exploration projects. Additionally, helicopters are indispensable for wildlife surveys, offering researchers a safe and efficient way to study Arctic species, including polar bears and caribou, across vast and inaccessible areas.

The Northern Lights

The best spots for viewing the Northern Lights in Grise Fiord are just outside the community, where the open skies provide uninterrupted views. The surrounding tundra and fjords create a stunning backdrop, with the vibrant greens, purples, and pinks of the auroras reflected off snow-covered landscapes and icy waters. For adventurous visitors, hiking to slightly elevated areas nearby can offer even more dramatic vistas of the auroral displays.

  • Timing Matters: The auroras are most active during the peak winter months from November to March, particularly on clear, moonless nights..
  • Dress Warmly: Arctic temperatures can be extreme, so wear insulated clothing, layers, and proper winter gear to stay comfortable..
  • Be Patient: The Northern Lights can be unpredictable. Stay outside for an extended period to increase your chances of seeing them..
  • Capture the Moment: Bring a camera with manual settings to photograph the auroras. Use a tripod for long exposures to capture the vivid colors and movements.

Winter Weather Conditions

Winter in Grise Fiord spans from late October to April, with temperatures often plunging to -35°C to -40°C (-31°F to -40°F) or lower, not accounting for wind chill. The cold is severe, but the dry Arctic air can make it feel less harsh than humid climates. During the height of winter, Grise Fiord experiences polar night, where the sun does not rise above the horizon for approximately three months, leaving the area bathed in twilight or complete darkness.

Summer Weather Conditions

Summers in Grise Fiord, from June to August, are short but offer milder conditions. Temperatures range from 0°C to 5°C (32°F to 41°F), with occasional spikes to 10°C (50°F) on warmer days. The summer months also bring 24-hour daylight, known as the midnight sun, creating a unique and surreal environment for exploring the Arctic tundra, fjords, and wildlife.
