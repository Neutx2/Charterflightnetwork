Location of Baker Lake
We Arrange both Jet and Turbo Prop Aircraft

Baker Lake, Nunavut, is a vibrant Arctic community located on the western shores of Baker Lake, the only inland Inuit settlement in Canada. Surrounded by tundra landscapes and stunning natural beauty, this remote destination is known for its rich Inuit culture, unique wildlife, and vast outdoor adventures. The area offers visitors a rare chance to explore the true Arctic environment, away from the bustling tourist paths. As part of the Kivalliq region, Baker Lake is a gateway to exploring the untouched wilderness of the Canadian Arctic, with its pristine lakes, rivers, and rugged landscapes making it a top choice for adventurers and cultural enthusiasts alike.

The Baker Lake Airport

Baker Lake Airport (CYBK) serves as the primary air transportation hub for the community and surrounding areas. This small but essential airport provides direct access to Baker Lake, making it a key link for charter flights and scheduled services. Featuring   a gravel runway, the airport handles both passenger and cargo flights, catering to residents, business travelers, and tourists alike. The terminal offers basic services and provides a convenient starting point for visitors heading out on their Arctic adventures. Customized private flights to Baker Lake make travel to this remote destination accessible, providing flexible options for direct routes from major cities in Canada.

Baker Lake Outdoor Adventures

Baker Lake offers a wide range of outdoor adventures, perfect for those seeking to explore the Arctic wilderness. Summer activities include kayaking, canoeing, and hiking across the vast tundra landscapes. Visitors can fish in the pristine waters of Baker Lake, known for its Arctic char and trout. Winter brings the opportunity to experience thrilling snowmobile tours and guided dog sledding expeditions. Adventurers can also explore traditional Inuit hunting grounds and learn about the region’s rich cultural heritage. From bird watching to exploring ancient archaeological sites, Baker Lake is a prime destination for nature lovers and those interested in the unique traditions of the Inuit people

Viewing the Northern Lights

Baker Lake is a great location to witness the awe-inspiring Northern Lights. During the long, dark winter nights, the sky often comes alive with vibrant displays of green, pink, and purple auroras dancing across the horizon. The remote location and clear Arctic air provide optimal conditions for viewing this natural phenomenon. Local guides offer Northern Lights tours, giving visitors the chance to experience the beauty of the aurora borealis while learning about the Inuit legends associated with the lights. For photographers and nature enthusiasts, this is an unforgettable experience and a highlight of any visit to Baker Lake.

Daylight Hours in Summer and Winter

Baker Lake experiences extreme variations in daylight throughout the year. In the summer months, the region enjoys nearly 24 hours of daylight, allowing for extended outdoor activities and exploration. This period, known as the Midnight Sun, is perfect for enjoying the endless Arctic landscapes. In contrast, winter brings long nights, with only a few hours of daylight. This extended darkness creates ideal conditions for viewing the Northern Lights and offers a magical winter experience under the Arctic sky.

Wildlife Viewing in Baker Lake

Baker Lake is a fantastic destination for wildlife enthusiasts. The surrounding tundra and waters are home to a variety of Arctic species, including caribou, Arctic foxes, and migratory birds like snow geese and tundra swans. In the summer, visitors can spot marine life such as seals and even beluga whales in nearby waters. Guided tours offer the chance to see polar bears in their natural habitat, making for a thrilling wildlife viewing experience. Whether by land or water, Baker Lake offers unparalleled opportunities for spotting unique Arctic animals.

Polar Bear Tours

Baker Lake offers the opportunity to witness one of the Arctic’s most iconic animals—the polar bear. Guided tours provide a safe and exciting way to observe these majestic creatures in their natural environment. Local Inuit guides lead the excursions, sharing their deep knowledge of polar bear behavior and Arctic wildlife. Tours are available during the summer and fall months when the bears are most active along the coastlines. It’s a must-do experience for wildlife enthusiasts visiting Baker Lake.

Weather and Temperatures in Summer

During the summer months, Baker Lake experiences milder temperatures ranging from 5°C to 15°C (41°F to 59°F). Although the weather can be relatively mild, the Arctic climate can be unpredictable, so visitors should be prepared for sudden changes. The summer season offers clear skies and long daylight hours, perfect for outdoor activities like hiking, fishing, and kayaking. Lightweight, warm layers and waterproof gear are recommended to stay comfortable while exploring.
