About Churchill Falls Newfoundland / Labrador

Churchill Falls is located in central Labrador and is the site of the Churchill Falls hydroelectric power generating station - known as the 2nd largest underground power station in the world. The local airport is a public use airport, which is owned and operated by the Churchill Falls Labrador Corporartion. The single aslphalt runway is 5,500 feet long - making it accessible to virtually all the charter aircraft operating in the region.

Churchill Falls is a hydroelectric power station and a community located in Labrador, Newfoundland and Labrador, Canada.

Location:

Churchill Falls is situated on the Churchill River in central Labrador. It is approximately 1,000 kilometers northwest of St. John's, the capital of Newfoundland and Labrador.

Hydroelectric Power Station:

Churchill Falls is best known for its hydroelectric power station, which is one of the largest in the world. The station harnesses the power of the Churchill River to generate electricity, with a total installed capacity of over 5,400 megawatts. The station began operating in 1971 and is jointly owned by Newfoundland and Labrador Hydro and Hydro-Québec.

Churchill Falls Agreement:

The development of the Churchill Falls hydroelectric project was made possible by the Churchill Falls Agreement, signed in 1969 between the government of Newfoundland and Labrador and Hydro-Québec. The agreement is often cited as one of the most controversial in Canadian history due to its long-term implications for electricity pricing and revenue sharing between the two parties.

Economic Impact:

The Churchill Falls hydroelectric project has had a significant economic impact on Newfoundland and Labrador, providing employment opportunities, revenue, and economic development in the region. However, the benefits of the project have been a subject of debate, particularly regarding the terms of the Churchill Falls Agreement and the division of profits between Newfoundland and Labrador and Hydro-Québec.

Community Life:

In addition to the hydroelectric power station, Churchill Falls is also a community that houses workers and their families associated with the operation of the power station. The community includes residential areas, recreational facilities, and other amenities to support the needs of residents.

Transportation:

Churchill Falls is accessible by road and air. The Trans-Labrador Highway provides road access to the community, connecting it to other towns and cities in Labrador and Newfoundland. Additionally, Churchill Falls Airport provides air service to other parts of the province.

Tourism:

While primarily known for its hydroelectric power station, Churchill Falls also attracts visitors interested in exploring the surrounding natural beauty of central Labrador. Outdoor recreational activities such as hiking, fishing, and wildlife viewing are popular among tourists visiting the area.

Overall, Churchill Falls is a significant site both for its hydroelectric power generation capabilities and its role in the economic and social landscape of Newfoundland and Labrador. It remains an important symbol of energy development and resource utilization in Canada.
