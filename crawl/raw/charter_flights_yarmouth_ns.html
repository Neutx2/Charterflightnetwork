About Yarmouth Nova Scotia - Wheel Plane Access

Discover The Convenience and Affordability of Flying Direct to or From Yarmouth Nova Scotia In The Speed Comfort and Security of A Private Charter Flight Yarmouth is located in the south western tip of Nova Scotia which is at the entrance to the Bay of Fundy. The town's 6,700 residents are pretty much tied to the fishing industry which is located in the heart of the world's largest lobster population. The local public airport, which is an airport of entry, has two asphalt runways, 6,000 and 5,000 feet long.

History:

Yarmouth has a rich maritime history dating back centuries. Originally inhabited by the indigenous Mi'kmaq people, the area was later settled by French Acadians in the 17th century and then by English settlers in the 18th century. The town flourished as a major seaport and fishing community, with shipbuilding and trade playing significant roles in its economy. Yarmouth was also a hub for the shipbuilding industry during the age of sail. Today, the town preserves its heritage through historic sites, museums, and cultural events.

Culture:

Yarmouth is known for its rich cultural heritage, influenced by its Acadian, Mi'kmaq, and English roots. The town hosts various cultural events and festivals throughout the year, celebrating its maritime heritage, music, and arts. Traditional seafood dishes, including lobster and scallops, are staples of Yarmouth's culinary scene, reflecting its coastal location and fishing traditions.

Activities:

Fishing and Boating: Yarmouth's coastal waters offer excellent opportunities for fishing and boating, with charters available for deep-sea fishing, lobster fishing, and sightseeing cruises.
Beachcombing: Yarmouth's beaches are perfect for beachcombing and leisurely walks along the shoreline, with sandy stretches and rocky coves waiting to be explored
Cultural Experiences: Explore Yarmouth's museums, galleries, and historic sites to learn about the town's maritime history, Acadian heritage, and Mi'kmaq culture. Highlights include the Yarmouth County Museum and Archives, the Firefighters' Museum of Nova Scotia, and the Art Gallery of Nova Scotia's Yarmouth branch.
Cape Forchu: This scenic headland is home to the iconic Cape Forchu Lighthouse and offers walking trails, picnic areas, and stunning views of the coastline.
Yarmouth Waterfront: The waterfront area is bustling with activity, featuring shops, restaurants, and attractions such as the Yarmouth Boat Basin and Mariners Centre..
Ellenwood Lake Provincial Park: Located just outside of Yarmouth, this provincial park offers camping, hiking, swimming, and picnicking opportunities in a beautiful natural setting.
Atlantic Canada's Only Car and Passenger Ferry Service to the USA: The ferry service from Yarmouth to Bar Harbor, Maine, operated by Bay Ferries Limited, provides a convenient and scenic way to travel between Nova Scotia and the United States, with onboard amenities and stunning views along the route.

Overall, Yarmouth offers a unique blend of maritime history, natural beauty, and cultural attractions, making it a must-visit destination for travelers exploring Nova Scotia's scenic South Shore.
