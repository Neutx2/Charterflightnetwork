Location of Gjoa Haven
We Arrange both Jet and Turbo Prop Charter Flights

Gjoa Haven, a small hamlet in Nunavut, Canada, is located on King William Island in the central Canadian Arctic Archipelago. It sits on the southeastern shore of the island, near the Rae Strait, a narrow channel that separates King William Island from the mainland. The hamlet is named after the ship Gjøa, used by Norwegian explorer Roald Amundsen during his successful expedition through the Northwest Passage in the early 1900s.

Gjoa Haven lies approximately 1,220 kilometers northwest of Iqaluit, the capital of Nunavut, and about 2,200 kilometers north of Winnipeg, Manitoba. Travel to the community is generally by air, with flights connecting from larger hubs like Yellowknife or Rankin Inlet. The surrounding geography is stark yet captivating, characterized by a tundra landscape with rolling, rocky terrain dotted by low shrubs, mosses, and lichens. The area is treeless, offering unobstructed views of the Arctic horizon, often accented by the shimmering northern lights in winter. The terrain is interspersed with numerous small lakes and ponds, created by glacial activity thousands of years ago.

About Gjoa Haven and Region

The nearby Rae Strait is part of the Arctic Ocean and remains frozen for much of the year. During the brief summer months, ice breaks up, creating open water that allows for limited marine travel. This waterway has a rich history, being central to the voyages of explorers seeking the Northwest Passage.

The surrounding waters support a range of Arctic marine life, including seals, beluga whales, and fish species such as Arctic char. The area is also home to migratory birds and iconic Arctic wildlife, including caribou and polar bears. Gjoa Haven is a hub for Inuit culture, history, and storytelling, offering visitors and residents a profound connection to the land and sea.

The Gjoa Haven Airport

The airport features a single gravel runway, designated 13/31, which measures 1,219 meters (4,000 feet) in length. This runway is capable of accommodating small to medium-sized aircraft, such as turboprops and regional jets, which are well-suited for Arctic conditions. The types of aircraft typically operating at Gjoa Haven Airport include De Havilland Canada Dash 8s and ATRs for passenger services, as well as various cargo aircraft, including Boeing 737 freighters and smaller planes for supply deliveries.

Passenger facilities at the airport are modest but functional, with a small terminal building that provides seating, basic amenities, and a check-in area. While there are no large-scale commercial services like restaurants or shops, the terminal ensures a comfortable space for passengers awaiting flights. The airport is operational year-round and accommodates day and night flights, but operations are subject to weather conditions, including visibility and strong winds common in the Arctic.

Experience the Aurora Borealis in Gjoa Haven, Nunavut

To make the most of your aurora-viewing experience, dress warmly in layered clothing, as temperatures can drop significantly during the peak viewing season. Bring a good-quality camera with a tripod to capture long-exposure shots of the lights dancing across the sky. Local guides are available to enhance your experience, offering tips on aurora photography and insight into the Inuit stories and legends surrounding the Northern Lights.

For an even more immersive adventure, consider combining your aurora-viewing trip with other Arctic activities, such as dog sledding or camping under the stars. With its remote location and spectacular displays, Gjoa Haven is a must-visit destination for anyone seeking the magic of the Aurora Borealis.

Inuit Culture & Heritage in Gjoa Haven, Nunavut

Gjoa Haven, known as the “Place of Plenty,” is a rich cultural hub that offers visitors a deep connection to Inuit heritage and traditions. The Inuit people have thrived in the Arctic for thousands of years, developing skills and practices essential for survival in one of the world’s harshest environments. These include traditional hunting, fishing, and tool-making techniques, many of which are still practiced and passed down through generations.

Inuit art plays a significant role in preserving and sharing their culture. Gjoa Haven is home to talented artists who create stunning sculptures, intricate prints, and handcrafted jewelry inspired by the Arctic landscape and wildlife. Carvings made from stone and bone often depict seals, polar bears, and other iconic animals, while prints and jewelry reflect traditional stories and modern interpretations of Inuit life.

The Inuit language, Inuktitut, is a cornerstone of cultural identity in Gjoa Haven. Visitors can hear this unique language spoken in daily life and learn words that connect them to the land and people. Storytelling, an essential tradition, is expressed in both Inuktitut and visual art, offering a glimpse into Inuit legends and history.

Exploring the Nattilik Heritage Centre in Gjoa Haven provides further insight into the community’s history, including exhibits on traditional practices, the Inuit relationship with explorers, and the enduring connection to the land. Gjoa Haven invites you to experience the resilience, artistry, and profound spirit of Inuit culture firsthand.
