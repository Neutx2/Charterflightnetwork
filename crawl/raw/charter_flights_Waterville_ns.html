About Waterville Nova Scotia - Wheel Plane Charter Access

Discover The Convenience and Affordability of Flying Direct to or From Waterville Nova Scotia In The Speed Comfort and Security of A Private Charter Flight Waterville is located on the Cornwallis Rivver, in west central Nova Scotia, just inland from the northeastern shores of the Bay of Fundy. You can charter flights direct to the local public use airport located adjacent to the town. The airport has a single 3,498 asphalt runway and sees over 6,000 aircraft movements each year.

History:

Waterville has a history rooted in agriculture and rural life. The area was originally inhabited by the indigenous Mi'kmaq people before European settlers arrived in the 18th century. The community grew as a farming settlement, with agriculture becoming the primary industry in the region. Waterville's rich agricultural heritage is reflected in its landscape, with farms, orchards, and vineyards dotting the countryside.

Economy:

Waterville's economy is predominantly based on agriculture, with farming, fruit growing, and winemaking being major contributors. The fertile soils of the Annapolis Valley support a variety of crops, including apples, berries, grapes, and vegetables. The region is known for its apple orchards and vineyards, producing a range of wines and ciders that are popular with locals and visitors alike. Additionally, Waterville benefits from tourism, as travelers come to explore the scenic beauty of the Annapolis Valley and enjoy outdoor recreational activities.

Culture:

Waterville's culture is influenced by its rural setting and close-knit community. The area has a strong sense of community spirit, with residents actively involved in local events, festivals, and activities. Traditional music, crafts, and culinary traditions are celebrated, with opportunities for residents and visitors to participate in cultural events and workshops throughout the year.

Activities:

Wine Tasting: Visitors can explore the local wineries and vineyards in the Annapolis Valley, sampling a variety of wines and ciders produced from locally grown grapes and fruits.
Farm Visits: Many farms in the Waterville area offer tours and farm experiences, allowing visitors to learn about agriculture, meet farm animals, and pick fresh produce.
Cultural Events: Waterville and neighboring communities host a variety of cultural events and festivals throughout the year, celebrating music, dance, arts, and local heritage. Visitors can enjoy live music performances, craft markets, and food festivals showcasing the region's culinary delights.
Annapolis Valley: The Annapolis Valley is a scenic region known for its picturesque landscapes, historic sites, and agricultural heritage. Visitors can explore charming towns, visit museums and heritage sites, and enjoy panoramic views of the countryside.
Grand-Pré National Historic Site: Located nearby in the town of Wolfville, this UNESCO World Heritage Site commemorates the Acadian settlement of Grand-Pré and the deportation of the Acadian people by the British in the 18th century. The site features interpretive exhibits, walking trails, and a memorial church.
Blomidon Provincial Park: This coastal park offers hiking trails, scenic lookouts, and opportunities for beachcombing and wildlife viewing. Visitors can explore the rugged coastline, cliffs, and tidal flats along the Bay of Fundy, known for having the highest tides in the world.

Overall, Waterville offers a peaceful retreat for visitors seeking to experience the natural beauty, agricultural traditions, and rural charm of the Annapolis Valley region.
