# What does a charter flight cost?

The honest answer: it depends — mostly on distance, aircraft class, and when
and where you're flying. Nobody can tell you a real price without knowing the
trip. But "it depends" shouldn't mean "no idea," so here is how the price is
built and what the wider market publishes as typical ranges.

## What drives the price

- **Distance and flight time** — most charter pricing is built on the
  aircraft's flying time, including any positioning to reach you.
- **Aircraft class** — a float plane to a fishing lodge and a jet to Toronto
  are different machines with very different hourly costs.
- **Positioning** — if the aircraft must fly empty to your departure point,
  that time is part of the trip. This is also why
  [empty legs](/empty-legs) are discounted: someone else already paid for the
  repositioning.
- **Season and demand** — peak fishing/hunting season in the north, holiday
  weeks, and events tighten availability.
- **Airport and route specifics** — remote strips, water landings, fuel
  availability and crew duty limits all shape the quote.

## Market ranges published by charter industry sources

Industry pricing guides published by charter brokers put typical **hourly**
rates in these bands (USD, North America; sources below):

- **Turboprops and light jets** — roughly $1,300–$3,500/hr (turboprop) and
  from ~$2,500/hr (light jet).
- **Mid-size jets** — roughly $3,200–$6,500/hr.
- **Large/heavy jets** — roughly $5,500–$12,500/hr.

Sources: BLADE "Private Jet Cost" guide; BlackJet charter cost calculator
guide; Haute Jets pricing guide. Ranges vary by source and change over time —
they are anchors, not quotes.
