# How to choose a charter operator

Chartering an aircraft in Canada is a well-regulated activity — but not all
operators are the same, and it's worth knowing what the paperwork means and
what to ask.

## What certification means

- [VERIFY vs Transport Canada / CARs] Commercial air services in Canada
  require an **Air Operator Certificate (AOC)** issued by Transport Canada,
  under the Canadian Aviation Regulations (CARs). Charter operations fall
  under [VERIFY: CARs Subpart 703 (air taxi), 704 (commuter) as applicable].
- [VERIFY] An AOC ties the operator to approved aircraft types, operating
  areas, maintenance control and crew training requirements.
- [VERIFY] Commercial operators must carry passenger liability insurance
  [VERIFY: CARs/Transport Canada minimums by seat count].

## Questions worth asking any operator

These are the questions experienced charter customers ask — none of them are
rude, and good operators answer them happily:

1. Is the flight operating under your own AOC, and in which operations
   category?
2. What aircraft will fly my trip, and how is it maintained?
3. What is the pilot's experience on this aircraft type and on this kind of
   route (floats, gravel, remote strips)?
4. What happens if weather delays or cancels the flight?
5. What exactly does the quoted price include — positioning, fuel surcharges,
   dock/landing fees, standby time?
6. What is your passenger liability coverage?
