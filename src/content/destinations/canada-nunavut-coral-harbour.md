Coral Harbour is a picturesque community located on Southampton Island in Nunavut, Canada. ### Coral Harbour Airport

Coral Harbour Airport (IATA: YZS) is a vital transportation hub serving the remote community of Coral Harbour, Nunavut, located on Southampton Island in the Canadian Arctic. The airport plays a crucial role in connecting this northern community to other parts of Nunavut and beyond, supporting both passenger travel and essential cargo transportation.

The airport features a gravel runway measuring approximately 5,000 feet (1,524 meters) in length, which accommodates various types of aircraft, including smaller regional planes used for passenger services and larger cargo planes delivering goods and supplies. Scheduled flights to and from the airport are primarily operated by airlines such as Calm Air and Air North, while private charters frequently utilize the airport for flexible travel and specialized transport needs.

### Weather & Suggested Clothing

Coral Harbour experiences extreme Arctic weather, with distinct conditions in summer and winter. Summers, from June to August, are short and cool, with temperatures ranging from 5°C to 15°C (41°F to 59°F). While the midnight sun provides long daylight hours, chilly winds and occasional rain are common, making layered clothing essential. Lightweight but warm jackets, water-resistant gear, and sturdy boots are ideal for summer adventures.

Winters in Coral Harbour, lasting from October to May, are harsh and cold, with temperatures often dipping below -30°C (-22°F) and wind chills making it feel even colder. Darkness dominates much of the day during the peak winter months, but the aurora borealis frequently lights up the night sky. For winter visits, pack heavy-duty winter gear, including insulated jackets, snow pants, thermal layers, and windproof gloves. Warm hats, scarves, and waterproof boots with good traction are vital for safety and comfort in icy conditions.

### Inuit Culture & Activities

Coral Harbour offers visitors a unique opportunity to immerse themselves in the rich traditions and culture of the Inuit people. Known locally as "Salliq," the community is deeply rooted in its heritage, and visitors are welcomed to experience the way of life that has sustained the Inuit for generations.

One of the highlights of a visit to Coral Harbour is participating in Inuit cultural activities. Traditional throat singing performances, drumming, and storytelling sessions offer a glimpse into the oral history and artistic expression of the community. Elders often share captivating tales about the land, animals, and survival in the Arctic, providing an authentic and heartfelt connection to Inuit culture.
